# Focus Galaxy — Product Positioning Brief

**Status:** Internal working brief · 4 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Canonical source for future article, landing page, LinkedIn, GitHub, and video work. This is product strategy, not final launch copy.

## Canonical product identity and messaging hierarchy

Use this hierarchy as the reusable source for downstream materials. The value and emotional outcomes are intended, not established results.

- **One-line product definition:** Focus Galaxy is a **visual priority map** and spatial prioritization product that places spatial priorities around **YOU / NOW**, representing importance, urgency, and energy / effort together.
- **Core user problem (hypothesis):** For some people with several competing commitments, a linear list makes it hard to compare priorities in relation to each other and choose where to focus now.
- **Core product hypothesis:** For visually oriented people handling several parallel commitments, a spatial map that represents importance, urgency, and energy / effort—and makes Focus Score and Focus Signal understandable—will help them compare trade-offs and choose a next focus with greater clarity than their current list-first approach, without sacrificing the precision they need to act.
- **Primary value proposition (intended):** See spatial priorities together, compare their importance, urgency, and energy / effort, and make a next-focus choice. Use existing task-management tools for execution.
- **Emotional value (intended, unmeasured):** Perspective and agency: “I can see what is pulling at me, and I can choose what to give attention to.”
- **Deliberately does not try to do:** Replace a to-do list, calendar, or project-management system; manage every execution detail; or decide what matters on a person's behalf.

## Evidence boundary

- **Demonstrated:** The project began as a roughly 40-minute prototype at a Replit event and was demonstrated there. The founder reports positive reactions and conversations; these were informal, not structured user research. The documented product is a browser-based visual priority map: importance, urgency, and energy / effort shape spatial priorities; a deterministic, rule-based Focus Score (0–100) ranks them; Focus Signal presents a concise ranked summary; priorities can be edited and saved locally.
- **Not demonstrated:** There is no evidence here of repeat use, improved focus or productivity, retention, willingness to pay, or demand from a defined segment. Event reactions do not establish those outcomes.
- **Hypotheses below:** Treat the audience, value, and use cases as propositions to investigate, not validated findings.
- **Future opportunities:** Directions worth considering only if user evidence supports them; they are not current product promises.

## Future opportunities

These are conditional ideas, not current product promises. If users return for reviews, explore a history of how spatial priorities shift. If users choose a focus in the map but execute elsewhere, explore sending selected priorities to an existing task or calendar tool. If facilitators naturally use the map with others, investigate a shared review mode. None of these needs is established today.

## Current strategic role

The immediate objective is not monetization. Focus Galaxy is simultaneously a real, evolving product experiment and public **Proof of Work** demonstrating product judgment, UX reasoning, technical execution, iteration, and productization. Keep the product as the subject: show those capabilities through its decisions and evolution, without turning this positioning into a personal-brand narrative. A polished prototype demonstrates execution; it does not establish user demand or outcomes.

## 1. Underlying user problem

People with several worthwhile, competing commitments must repeatedly decide what deserves attention now. A conventional list can capture and order work, but a long queue may make the relative importance, urgency, and energy / effort of different priorities hard to compare. The problem is not that lists are inherently poor; it is that a linear view may be a weak fit for some spatial prioritization moments.

## 2. Behavioral insight behind spatial prioritization

**Hypothesis:** Some people may compare spatial priorities more easily when they can see them in relation to one another. A visual priority map can make relative weight, proximity, and activity visible together. “YOU / NOW” gives the map a personal reference point: the question is what is drawing attention in this moment, not merely what is next in a queue.

This is a design rationale, not a cognitive-science claim. Space and motion can also add visual noise. The product must show that the map improves orientation for its intended users before claiming a general advantage.

## 3. Who may find it most useful

The strongest initial fit is likely people who manage several parallel personal or work priorities, make frequent trade-offs, and prefer visual over purely sequential planning. They need an overview for comparing spatial priorities and deciding where to focus; they do not necessarily need another system for managing every task detail.

Focus Galaxy is less likely to help people whose main need is strict sequencing, deadlines, dependencies, team assignment, or high-volume task capture. A list, calendar, or project tool may serve those jobs better.

## 4. Provisional personas

These are research starting points, not research findings.

- **Primary — The visually oriented solo operator:** A knowledge worker, independent creator, or student with several active commitments. They revisit priorities as circumstances change and want a quick way to compare what matters, what is time-sensitive, and what will take effort.
- **Secondary — The reflective planner:** Someone who plans in review sessions rather than maintaining a detailed task system throughout the day. They want to step back, notice competing priorities, and choose what to protect, defer, or break down.

Validate the underlying behaviors before narrowing these into demographic segments or treating job titles as evidence of fit.

## 5. Jobs To Be Done

- When several priorities compete for my attention, help me compare them and choose what to focus on next.
- When importance, urgency, or energy / effort changes, help me update the picture and understand why a spatial priority appears more or less prominent.
- When I step back to review my work or life, help me see what is competing for attention and decide what to protect, defer, or simplify.

These are candidate jobs to test in interviews and observed product use.

## 6. Why spatial prioritization may help versus a normal task list

A list is strong at sequence, scanning, deadlines, and execution. A visual priority map may be stronger at showing spatial priorities together: the user is the reference point, while size, distance, and activity express selected attributes including importance, urgency, and energy / effort. The model makes spatial prioritization itself the object of interaction, rather than asking the user to infer every relationship from rows.

The visual priority map should complement execution tools, not claim to replace them. Keep labels, controls, the rule-based Focus Score, and Focus Signal understandable; provide a usable path for people who do not benefit from motion or spatial navigation. Whether users actually decide faster or with more confidence remains unproven.

## 7. What Focus Galaxy should not become

- An all-purpose project-management suite with tasks, chat, documents, calendars, and team administration competing for attention.
- A gamified streak or reward system that makes planning itself the work.
- An authoritative “answer engine” that tells people what matters without showing its assumptions.
- A pseudo-scientific productivity score or a promise to eliminate overwhelm.
- A crowded collection of integrations before the core map is useful on its own.

## 8. Strongest product differentiation

The strongest candidate is the **visual priority map of spatial priorities**: priorities orbit a “YOU / NOW” center, while importance, urgency, and energy / effort inform their visual form and deterministic Focus Score. Focus Signal turns those scores into a concise ranked summary. The combination gives the product a legible point of view: make competing attention visible, then let the person decide.

The galaxy aesthetic alone is not a defensible difference, and market uniqueness has not been established. The product must earn distinction through clarity, useful decisions, and repeat use.

## 9. Core product hypothesis

For visually oriented people handling several parallel commitments, a visual priority map that represents importance, urgency, and energy / effort—and makes Focus Score and Focus Signal understandable—will help them compare trade-offs and choose a next focus with greater clarity than their current list-first approach, without sacrificing the precision they need to act.

This can be tested against a list-based view using real prioritization tasks. Observe comprehension, time to a choice, confidence, and whether people return to the map; do not infer product value from visual appeal alone.

## 10. Emotional value

The intended feeling is perspective and agency: “I can see what is pulling at me, and I can choose what to give attention to.” Calm, curiosity, and a sense of possibility are desired outcomes, not measured effects. The experience should make spatial priorities feel orientable, not turn unfinished work into a source of guilt.

## 11. Potential real-world use cases

These are plausible scenarios to explore, not validated demand:

- A daily check-in to choose among several competing work priorities.
- A weekly review to decide what to protect, defer, or break into smaller steps.
- A student balancing coursework, exam preparation, and personal commitments.
- A creator or independent worker comparing parallel projects before planning the next work session.
- A personal reset after new requests or changing deadlines disrupt an existing plan.

## 12. Product language to use

- **Visual priority map** as the product category; **spatial priorities** for priorities represented in the map.
- **YOU / NOW** for the map's personal reference point.
- **Importance**, **urgency**, and **energy / effort** for the dimensions represented.
- **Focus Score** for the deterministic, rule-based numerical score used to rank priorities.
- **Focus Signal** for the concise summary and ranked-priority view based on Focus Scores.
- “Designed to help you choose what to focus on next.”

Describe the visual encoding plainly. A Focus Score is a comparison cue, not objective truth; Focus Signal is a rule-based summary, not a prediction or an authority. Say the map represents priorities; do not imply it reads a person's mind or knows their full context.

## 13. Language and claims to avoid

- “AI-powered,” “smart prioritization,” or “the app knows what matters” — the documented scoring is rule-based, not AI-driven.
- “Science-backed,” “proven to improve focus,” “boosts productivity,” or “eliminates overwhelm” — no supporting outcome research is available.
- “The future of productivity,” “revolutionary,” or “the only visual task manager” — generic or unsupported superiority claims.
- “Replaces your to-do list” or “one system for everything” — Focus Galaxy is for spatial prioritization and orientation, not comprehensive task management or execution.
- Treating event reactions, a working prototype, or a hosted demo as proof of sustained demand or product-market fit.

## Canonical Messaging for Public Surfaces

- **Hero-level description:** “Focus Galaxy is a visual priority map that places spatial priorities around YOU / NOW, making importance, urgency, and energy / effort visible together.”
- **Supporting messages:**
  - “Compare competing priorities in one spatial view.”
  - “Focus Score is a deterministic, rule-based ranking cue; Focus Signal presents a concise ranked summary.”
  - “Choose a next focus in Focus Galaxy; keep detailed task tracking and execution in the tools you already use.”
- **Approved product vocabulary:** visual priority map; spatial priorities; YOU / NOW; importance; urgency; energy / effort; Focus Score; Focus Signal.
- **Claims that must remain avoided:** AI-powered or predictive prioritization; scientifically proven or productivity-improving outcomes; elimination of overwhelm; market superiority or uniqueness; validated demand, retention, or product-market fit; replacing task-management or execution tools.

## 14. One-sentence positioning statement

Focus Galaxy is a visual priority map for people balancing competing commitments, designed to place spatial priorities around YOU / NOW and show how importance, urgency, and energy / effort relate so they can choose what to focus on next.

## 15. Product manifesto

Your priorities are connected, but a list can make them look like a queue. Focus Galaxy gives spatial priorities a shared space around YOU / NOW. It makes selected signals visible; it does not decide what your life should value. Focus Score is a cue with understandable rules, not an authority. Focus Signal summarizes those rules; it does not predict the right answer. The visual priority map should help you pause, compare, and choose, then get out of the way so you can act. We will earn every larger promise through observed use, not through the beauty of the galaxy or the speed of the first prototype.
